Section 02
How advisors get paid

All advisor compensation is revenue-based — no base salary. The payout rate is determined entirely by how a client relationship originated. This creates a tiered incentive structure that rewards both firm-assisted and independently developed business.

25%
Company-sourced clients
Leads and clients originated by the firm. Typically the majority of a new advisor's book — these are handed over as the advisor joins.
50%
Referrals & advisor-sourced
Children of existing clients, estate settlement referrals, and any clients the advisor brings entirely on their own.
25%
Account purchases
Partial practice or account-level acquisitions. Revenue credited to the advisor on an ongoing basis.
Incentive for Advisors: Once advisors reach the 150-200 household cap, their incentive remains as they can continue bringing on more of their own referrals and offload firm referrals to slowly bring their practice more towards the 50% payout vs. 25% payout.
Section 03
Hurdles

The team is only considered truly profitable when all three conditions below are met simultaneously. The firm's main costs are the associate advisor salary + book purchases costs — everything else must be self-funded by the practice.

Hurdle 1
Advisor income
Every advisor earns at or above $90K minimum, growing toward $150K+ at full book. No firm subsidy.
Hurdle 2
Practice self-sufficient
The book generates enough gross revenue to fully cover all advisor payouts. Firm net is positive.
Hurdle 3
Firm profitable
Firm net after all advisor payouts exceeds $80K — covering the associate salary with surplus remaining.

Investment Rating — Strong.
  • The $80K associate salary is the firm's primary recurring cost for running this team structure.
  • At just 65 NWAM Sourced households — roughly 43% of full capacity — NWAM already nets $190K annually, a 238% return on the salary investment.
  • At full book (150 HH), NWAM's annual net climbs to $544K per advisor — a 680% return.
  • With 5 advisors at capacity (each at 150 NWAM Sourced HH), the team generates over $3M in annual net revenue for NWAM against one fixed salary cost.
  • The 2–4 year ramp timeline is the primary variable — early book purchases and firm-seeded HH transfers directly accelerate the return.

What drives the difference

Bridging from the Practice Buyout Model's 10-year firm net to the Poloko Example's, one driver at a time.

Why the Poloko structure is more profitable — two causes:
  • NWAM keeps a bigger revenue share. In the Practice Buyout Model a standing 25% is carved out to the servicing advisors (dept) forever, so NWAM sees only 25% during the buyout and 75% after. In the Poloko deal NWAM retains 50% during the payout and 100% once it ends — servicing is just a flat salary cost, not a perpetual revenue share.
  • No separate purchase price. The Practice Buyout Model pays a lump-sum purchase price on top of the advisor's revenue stream. In the Poloko deal the 50% stream is the purchase price — there is no additional outlay, so the acquisition self-funds from the book's own revenue.
Section 06
When to add the next advisor

Adding an advisor too early creates a subsidy problem — they earn below the income floor and drag team profitability. The right trigger is when existing advisors have accumulated enough overflow households to seed a sustainable book from day one.

How seeding works: Each advisor has a soft cap (where overflow starts building) and a hard cap (where redistribution is required). The households between those two numbers are the pool available to seed a new hire. The question is whether that pool is large enough to sustain the new advisor immediately.
